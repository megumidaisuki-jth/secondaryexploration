## Discussion

### Principal interpretation

The primary interpretation must begin with the cross-phase decision, not with
the largest isolated point estimate. Insert one of the following evidence-led
statements after both phases pass strict replay:

- `[If at least one equal-family global contrast is independently confirmed in
  the beneficial direction: identify the endpoint and graph size, give the
  formal and confirmation adjusted intervals, and state the direction without
  extending it to unregistered sizes.]`
- `[If the corresponding adjusted intervals in both phases exclude zero in the
  harmful direction: report the repeated adverse direction explicitly. State
  that it does not satisfy the registered beneficial-confirmation state; do not
  call it no difference.]`
- `[If only one phase supports a beneficial direction: identify the state as
  formal-only or confirmation-only and do not describe it as independently
  confirmed.]`
- `[If phase directions are inconclusive or discordant: state that the study
  did not establish a phase-replicated direction for that contrast under the
  registered design; do not translate this into equivalence.]`

For each phase, a named NCH, FHS3, FHS5 or demand-aware interval is
confirmatory only when its corresponding global gate is open; a closed gate
makes that phase's named-family estimate descriptive. Cross-phase independent
confirmation additionally requires the named adjusted interval itself to be
beneficial in both phases. When both global gates are open but either named
interval fails, the registered confirmatory test does not support an
independently confirmed benefit and must not be relabelled as merely
descriptive. The normalized restricted no-path time and
fixed-horizon failure risk should be discussed together because they describe
different aspects of the same administratively censored service process. A
direction present for one endpoint but not the other is a feature to explain,
not a discrepancy to hide.

### What topology can and cannot explain

A beneficial independently confirmed global contrast would show that the equal
mean of the four registered HPN source-family contrasts shifts in the
registered beneficial direction relative to their resource-matched binary
references under common capital, traffic and routing rules in the tested
synthetic strata. Adjusted intervals that are harmful in both phases would
instead establish a repeated adverse direction without satisfying the frozen
beneficial-confirmation state. A confirmed named-family contrast would localize
the beneficial decision to its registered HPN construction and binary bracket.
None of these results would by itself identify a universal mechanism.
Interpretation should compare three bounded possibilities against the evidence:
route-set redundancy, directional-balance distribution, and coordination
burden. These are exploratory consistency checks rather than mechanism tests.
Use raw topology, balance-state and dynamic-cost witnesses only through a
separately frozen and strictly replayed descriptive projection; do not collapse
them into an unregistered scalar objective or claim causality from an
association.

For the demand-aware HPN, interpretation must also report whether topology
training actually changed the FHS5 seed in the relevant cells. A weak or absent
service contrast may reflect an inactive bounded search rather than evidence
that demand information is intrinsically unhelpful. Conversely, a changed
topology with a replicated service contrast would remain evidence about this
registered heuristic and training workload, not about a global optimum.

### Heterogeneity, scale and censoring

The equal weighting of ER-GNM, BA and fixed-count SBM strata enforces equal
stratum contribution even though the registered design is balanced at 20
parents per stratum. Discussion
of a global result must therefore be accompanied by the three model-specific
parent summaries and by an explicit note when directions are heterogeneous.
The four graph sizes are separate registered hierarchies; apparent monotonicity
across sizes is exploratory unless supported by an additional prespecified
trend analysis, which the current contract does not contain.

Administrative censoring limits what can be said about failure-time tails.
`q=0.10` contributes only an event-coverage and identifiability diagnostic, and
no numerical censored quantile is estimated even when the coverage condition
passes. The restricted-time endpoint keeps censored observations at the common
horizon, whereas fixed-horizon failure risk records whether path unavailability
was observed. Their joint interpretation must preserve this distinction.

### Relation to prior work

The final text should position the evidence against three adjacent lines of
work already established in the Introduction: balance-aware routing and
rebalancing, topology and capacity design, and multi-party HPN construction.
The contribution is not the invention of multi-party channels, hypergraph
routing, depletion time or rebalancing. It is the resource-matched stateful
comparison with held-out traffic, parent-level inference and a disjoint-seed
confirmation phase. Add direct comparisons only when the evaluated protocol,
resource constraint and endpoint are commensurable; otherwise describe the
relationship as complementary rather than superior.

### Limitations and external validity

Five boundaries must remain visible in the final Discussion. First, the formal
evidence comes from three registered synthetic parent-graph strata and does not
represent an unknown population of deployed payment networks. Second, the
experiment assumes complete residual-state information, exhaustive feasible
path search, atomic execution, fixed per-node capital and no rebalancing;
fees, latency, privacy leakage, concurrent payments and protocol-level failures
are outside the primary model. Third, demand-aware topology and capacity
allocation use bounded deterministic search rather than global optimization.
Fourth, nested held-out traces support within-parent paired comparisons but are
not independent network replicates; inference rests on independent parent
graphs. Fifth, Lightning samples provide structural cross-sections only. They
contain neither private balances nor realized payment demand, overlap across
anchors, and stay outside the confirmatory bootstrap. The 2026 panel remains
ineligible even for diagnostic publication unless its exact bytes can be
lawfully preserved in a durable repository. Preservation would satisfy that
publication gate only; the panel remains diagnostic and never enters the
synthetic confirmatory bootstrap.

These limits define concrete next tests: add routing and rebalancing protocol
sensitivities without changing the primary claim retrospectively; evaluate
alternative capital regimes and training budgets; and repeat the registered
comparison on lawfully preserved network snapshots with defensible balance and
demand models. Such extensions should be framed as new studies rather than as
post hoc repairs of the current confirmatory design.
